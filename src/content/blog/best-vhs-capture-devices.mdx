## Best cheap USB pick: Roxio Easy VHS to DVD 3 Plus

The [Roxio Easy VHS to DVD 3 Plus](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07TWN55VV) is the practical pick for someone who wants a USB capture dongle, a guided Windows workflow, and a price that does not feel like buying a second VCR. Roxio says the kit captures from RCA composite or S-Video through its USB device and supports Windows 11, 10, 8, and 7 SP1, with output options that include DVD, ISO, MPEG2, and AVC/H.264 ([Roxio](https://www.roxio.com/en/products/easy-vhs-to-dvd/standard/)).

This is not the fanciest capture path, and it is Windows-first. But for a lot of tapes, especially home movies, school plays, local commercials, camcorder chaos, and found-footage oddities, “good enough and actually done” beats “perfect and still sitting in a closet.”

### Strengths

- **Guided workflow**: The software is built for VHS-to-digital jobs, not just generic capture.
- **Useful inputs**: RCA composite and S-Video cover most VCRs and camcorders.
- **Good price**: At $56.92 when checked, it costs less than many “premium” USB dongles.
- **DVD or digital**: You can make a digital file or go old-school and burn a disc if your setup still supports that.

### Weak spots

- **Windows bias**: Mac users should look at Elgato or a standalone recorder instead.
- **Cables may not be included**: Roxio notes that RCA, S-Video, and audio cables are not included and should come from your VCR or camcorder setup ([Roxio](https://www.roxio.com/en/products/easy-vhs-to-dvd/standard/)).
- **Software matters**: If drivers are cranky, your capture day becomes a driver day.

### Pros and cons

| Pros | Cons |
|---|---|
| Affordable | Windows-focused |
| RCA and S-Video support | Extra cables may be needed |
| Good for beginners | Not the highest-end capture quality |
| Real Amazon page with usable product link | Less flexible than OBS-based setups |

## A quick setup checklist

- **VCR**: Use the best working VCR you can find, and clean the heads before blaming the capture device.
- **Video connection**: Use S-Video if your VCR has it, otherwise use the yellow RCA composite cable.
- **Audio connection**: Use red and white RCA cables for stereo audio. If your VCR only has one audio output, use a splitter.
- **Computer**: If using USB or PCIe capture, close other heavy apps before recording.
- **Storage**: Save captures to a drive with plenty of space. Old tapes are slow, but files add up fast.
