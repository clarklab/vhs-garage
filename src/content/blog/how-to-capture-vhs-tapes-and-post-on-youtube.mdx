Capturing VHS tapes does not need to be complicated. You need a working VCR, a simple USB capture device, the right cable, and a place to record the signal. From there, the real work is choosing the moments worth preserving and giving them enough context that people can actually find them.

Here is the basic VHS-to-YouTube workflow.

## 1. Get a USB capture device

Start with a USB video capture device that accepts analog video. Most people use a small USB dongle with RCA inputs. You do not need a studio rig to get started, but do get something your computer recognizes reliably.

If your VCR has cleaner outputs or you have a time base corrector, great. If not, do not let that stop you. The best capture setup is the one you will actually use.

## 2. Plug your VCR into the capture device

Connect the VCR to the USB capture device with RCA cables:

- Yellow for video
- White for left audio
- Red for right audio

Then plug the capture device into your computer. Open your system camera or video settings if you want to confirm the signal is coming through before recording.

## 3. Record clips at VHS Garage

Go to [vhsgarage.com/capture](/capture) in Chrome and use the VHS Garage capture station to record clips from your tape. The capture tool is built for quick sessions: preview the signal, record the good stuff, and add information while the tape is fresh in your head.

Short clips are often easier to publish than giant full-tape rips. Think commercials, local news segments, strange bumpers, home-recorded oddities, trailers, station IDs, and anything with a specific story attached.

## 4. Add useful details

Before you publish, add the context you know:

- Tape title or label
- Approximate year
- Source or location
- Show, movie, commercial, or station name
- Anything unusual about the clip

Those details help viewers, and they help search engines understand what the clip is. A video called "old tape part 4" is hard to discover. A video called "1993 Austin Local Commercial Break from VHS Recording" has a fighting chance.

## 5. Publish to YouTube with a strong title and thumbnail

When you are ready, publish the clip to YouTube. Use a clear SEO title that says what the footage actually is. VHS Garage Capture can help you shape that title and create a more useful thumbnail direction, so you are not starting from a blank box.

Good YouTube titles usually include the year, subject, format, and location when you know them. Good thumbnails should show the actual footage, not a generic tape graphic. If the frame is weird, local, specific, or nostalgic, let that be the hook.
