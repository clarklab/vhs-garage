Here is the annoying truth: most Amazon capture cards do either HDMI or analog, not HDMI, RCA, and S-Video beautifully in one tidy box. VHS people live in adapter land. Retro gaming people live in adapter land. Anyone trying to capture a VCR, a camcorder, a DVD player, and a mystery HDMI gadget from a drawer is basically running a small airport.

So this guide is not about the “one perfect card.” It is about the best mixed-input capture options that get closest to the job: HDMI for modern sources, RCA/composite for VCRs, S-Video when your deck gives you the cleaner analog output, and enough flexibility to work with a site like [VHS Garage Capture](https://vhsgarage.com/capture), which lets you select a capture device in Chrome, record clips, save them to disk, and publish to your own YouTube channel.

Prices were checked on Amazon on April 30, 2026 when Amazon showed them. For unavailable items, we kept the Amazon product page because it is still useful for specs, images, and future restock checks.

## Best all-in-one mixed-input recorder: ClearClick HD Video Capture Box Ultimate 3.0

The [ClearClick HD Video Capture Box Ultimate 3.0](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08XYGN2WM) is the closest thing here to a “just plug in whatever weird source you found” recorder. ClearClick says it records from HDMI, DVI, SDI, RCA/AV, VGA, YPbPr, and S-Video sources, saves to USB drives or SD cards, previews on a 7-inch LCD, and records up to 1080p60 from HD sources while recording standard-definition AV/RCA sources up to 720x480 ([ClearClick](https://www.clearclick.com/products/hd-video-capture-box-ultimate-3-0-record-and-stream-video-from-hdmi-dvi-sdi-rca-av-vga-ypbpr-video-sources-no-computer-required)).

### Strengths

- **Huge input spread**: HDMI, RCA/AV, S-Video, VGA, DVI, SDI, and component are all in the conversation.

## What we would avoid

- **HDMI-only capture sticks for VHS without a converter**: They cannot accept a VCR’s analog signal by themselves.
- **HDMI-to-RCA converters for capture**: For a VCR going into a computer, you usually need RCA-to-HDMI, not HDMI-to-RCA.
- **Mystery listings with no input photos**: If the product image does not clearly show the ports, assume the ports you need are missing.
- **Anything that promises to “make VHS HD”**: It can upscale the signal, but it cannot invent detail that was never on the tape.
