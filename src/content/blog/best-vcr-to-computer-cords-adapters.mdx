VCRs are simple until you try to connect one to a laptop made after people stopped saying “be kind, rewind.” Then suddenly you are staring at yellow, white, red, S-Video, HDMI, USB, a tiny power cable, and three adapters that all claim to be “AV.”

This guide is the cable drawer, organized. It covers the little pieces that make a VHS capture setup work: RCA switchers, RCA-to-HDMI converters, S-Video-to-HDMI converters, actual S-Video cables, and a capture device or two that gets the signal into the computer. Once the signal is visible, [VHS Garage Capture](https://vhsgarage.com/capture) is the fun part: select your capture device in Chrome, record clips, save them to disk, and publish to your own YouTube channel when the clip deserves a crowd.

## First: know what direction the signal is going

For digitizing VHS, the signal starts at the VCR and ends at your computer. That usually means one of these paths:

| VCR output | Adapter path | Computer input |
|---|---|---|
| Yellow/red/white RCA | USB analog capture device | USB |
| Yellow/red/white RCA | RCA-to-HDMI converter | HDMI capture device |
| S-Video plus red/white audio | S-Video-to-HDMI converter | HDMI capture device |
| S-Video plus red/white audio | USB capture device with S-Video | USB |

Do not buy an HDMI-to-RCA converter unless you are sending a modern HDMI device to an old TV. For VCR capture, you usually want RCA-to-HDMI or S-Video-to-HDMI.

## The cleanest VCR-to-computer recipes

### Cheapest workable route

Use a VCR with yellow/red/white RCA output, connect it to the [Roxio Easy VHS to DVD 3 Plus](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07TWN55VV), and record on a Windows computer. This avoids HDMI conversion and keeps the number of boxes low.

### Best Mac-friendly route

Use a VCR with RCA or S-Video output, connect it to the [Elgato Video Capture](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0029U2YSA), and record on Mac or PC. Use S-Video plus red/white RCA audio if the VCR supports it.

### Best HDMI-capture-card route

Use the [Dingsun S-Video to HDMI Converter](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07L931SB5) if your VCR has S-Video, or use the [ABLEWE RCA to HDMI Converter](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07RX69KR8) if your VCR only has yellow/red/white RCA. Then feed HDMI into your existing HDMI capture device.

### Best multi-source bench

Use the [Fosmon 4-Way RCA Switcher](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00HNCPR92) for several analog sources, then feed the output into a USB capture kit or an RCA-to-HDMI converter. Label the inputs because “VCR 1,” “VCR 2,” and “the haunted camcorder” all look the same at midnight.

## Final unplug

The right cable setup should feel boring. That is the goal. When the signal shows up, the audio is in sync, and the file saves where you expect, you can stop thinking about adapters and start watching the tape. Which is good, because the best part is always the thing nobody remembered recording.
